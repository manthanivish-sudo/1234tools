A classroom quiz game asks a question on the board, the pupils answer on devices, and the points decide a winner. Two tools here do that. What paid quiz platforms do that we do not

These are commonly the reasons a school or a training company pays for a quiz platform rather than using a free tool.

  • It has accounts, classes and reports over a term. Paid platforms typically tie answers to named pupils in a class and chart progress across weeks. Here pupils join with a nickname, and the only record is the CSV the teacher exports at the end of a session.
  • It offers a huge library of ready-made questions. Large shared question banks are common in the category. We have ready-made packs for Quiz Maker, written for this site, and you write your own in Live Presentation; there is no searchable library of other teachers’ quizzes.
  • It runs self-paced homework. Many platforms let pupils work through a quiz alone, in their own time, with the results waiting for the teacher. Both tools here are live: the teacher advances the questions, and a room closes when the session ends (24 hours at most).
  • It connects to the school’s learning platform. Roster import and grade return are commonly offered. Nothing here connects to another system; results leave as a CSV file.
  • It has avatars, music and a lot of polish. Commercial game platforms invest heavily in animation and rewards. Here the big screen is plain, with a podium and confetti at the end of a quiz.
  • It marks every kind of answer for you. In Live Presentation a quiz question is multiple choice and is scored by speed; open answers are collected but not marked. Quiz Maker can mark a typed answer you enter for a team, ignoring case, accents and punctuation, but it is the teacher who types it in.

When you should pay for a quiz platform

If quizzing is part of how a school or a company measures learning, one of the paid platforms is probably the right answer.

  • You need results by named pupil across a term, and reports for a head of department or a parent.
  • You want homework quizzes pupils complete on their own, at home.
  • You want a ready-made library of questions for every topic, shared across a staff room.
  • The school’s learning platform has to receive the grades, or the class list has to come in from it.
  • You run sessions for more than the launch-trial cap of 1,000 phones, or need a vendor with a service promise.

Frequently asked questions

How many pupils can play at once?

During the launch trial a free session takes up to 1,000 phones. When it is full, the next phone is told the session is full.

Do pupils need an account, an app or an email address?

No. They open 1234tools.com/join in their phone’s browser, type the six-digit code (or scan the QR code) and choose a nickname. The teacher needs no account either.

What happens to the pupils’ answers?

Answers pass through our server only to reach the presenter’s screen. They are held in memory for the room’s life and deleted when it closes (24 hours at most). No accounts, no cookies, no analytics for participants.

How are the points worked out in Live Presentation?

A right answer scores 1000 points if it arrives at once, falling evenly to 500 at the deadline; a wrong answer or no answer scores 0. The time is measured by our relay when the answer arrives. Players on equal points are ranked by the total time their right answers took, fastest first.

Can I run the quiz with no phones?

Yes, with Quiz Maker: host it on the board, enter each team’s answer, and print question and answer sheets. Phones are optional there.

Does it work without internet?

Not with phones. The quiz travels to the phones and the answers come back through our relay, so the teacher’s computer and every phone need a connection. Quiz Maker without phones opens offline after the first visit.
