Live polling software does one job: the presenter asks, the room answers on phones, and the totals appear on the big screen. The tools here do that job for multiple-choice polls, word clouds, 0-to-10 and star ratings, open answers and timed quiz questions, and they can be strung into one prepared deck with Live Presentation. Nothing needs installing, nobody signs in, and the presenter can export every result as a CSV file.

We compare against the category, not a product: we have not tested any particular polling service and will not describe one from memory. What paid polling software does that we do not

These are things the category commonly offers. They are the reasons a company, a school district or a conference organiser pays rather than uses a free tool.

  • It keeps your sessions for you. A paid product typically has an account where past polls, results and audiences live, so last term’s results are one click away. Here nothing is stored on our side: a deck is saved in the presenter’s own browser, and results survive only if the presenter presses Export CSV before ending the session.
  • It sits inside the slides you already have. Many paid products add polls to a slide deck made in other software, so the poll appears in the middle of the presentation. Live Presentation is its own deck: you write its slides in it (multiple choice, quiz, open answers, word cloud, rating, Q&A, leaderboard, a heading), and it does not import slides made elsewhere.
  • It takes bigger rooms, on a plan. Paid plans commonly advertise audience sizes and event support. During the launch trial a free session takes up to 1,000 phones. When it is full, the next phone is told the session is full. Check a vendor’s own limit for a room larger than that.
  • It knows who answered. Accounts, rosters and logins let a paid product tie answers to named people and report on them over a term. Here people join with a nickname, and the presenter’s page cannot tell you who a nickname really is.
  • It integrates and reports. Links to learning platforms, calendars and spreadsheets, and dashboards across many sessions, are common in paid products. Here the report is a CSV file made in the presenter’s browser, one per session.
  • It comes with support and a service promise. A paid plan usually comes with a named support route and an uptime commitment. These tools are free, in a launch trial, and carry no such promise.

When you should pay for polling software

If polling is a regular part of your work rather than an occasional session, one of the paid products is probably the right answer.

  • You run sessions for audiences larger than the launch-trial cap of 1,000 phones, or need a service promise for a conference.
  • You need to know which named person answered what, over a term or a programme, and report on it.
  • The polls must sit inside a deck that is made in other software and presented from there.
  • You want past sessions, audiences and reports kept for you in an account, without remembering to export a file.
  • You need integrations with a learning platform or a spreadsheet, or a named support contact.

Frequently asked questions

How many people can answer?

During the launch trial a free session takes up to 1,000 phones. When it is full, the next phone is told the session is full.

Do people need an app or an account?

No. They open 1234tools.com/join in their phone’s browser, type the six-digit code (or scan the QR code) and choose a nickname. The presenter needs no account either.

Who sees the answers, and what does your server keep?

Answers pass through our server only to reach the presenter’s screen. They are held in memory for the room’s life and deleted when it closes (24 hours at most). No accounts, no cookies, no analytics for participants. The counting is done in the presenter’s browser.

Does it work without an internet connection?

No. The votes travel from the phones to the presenter’s screen through our relay, so the presenter’s laptop and every phone need a connection. A session also ends by itself: a room is deleted when it closes, and at the latest after 24 hours.

Can I keep the results?

Yes. Export CSV saves every question in the session: votes and percentages, typed answers, word counts, ratings and quiz scores by nickname. The file is made in your browser, and nothing is kept by us once the session ends.

Can someone vote twice?

Not from the same phone. A second vote from the same phone connection replaces the first, or is refused if you turned changing votes off. A different phone or browser counts as a different person, so this is not an identity check.
