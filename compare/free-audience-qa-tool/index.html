What paid event Q&A tools do that we do not

These are commonly the reasons an events team or a communications department pays rather than uses a free tool.

  • It is built into the event platform. Paid event and webinar tools put questions beside the video, the agenda and the registration list. This tool is a separate page next to your slides or video call; the audience join it at 1234tools.com/join.
  • It lets a team moderate together. Moderation by several people, with roles, is common in the category. Here approval happens on the presenter’s page.
  • It knows who is asking. Registered attendees and verified names come with an event platform. Here people join with a nickname, and you cannot verify who a nickname is; the board can hide nicknames, but your own page still shows them.
  • It keeps events and archives in an account. Paid tools keep past events’ questions in an account. Here nothing is stored by us; the presenter exports a CSV before ending the session.
  • It takes bigger audiences, on a plan. Plans commonly advertise audience sizes. During the launch trial a free session takes up to 1,000 phones. When it is full, the next phone is told the session is full.
  • It comes with a support route and a service promise. A paid plan usually comes with a named support route. This tool is free, in a launch trial, and carries no such promise.

When you should pay for an event Q&A tool

If questions are one part of a larger event that you run again and again, a paid event platform is probably the right answer.

  • The questions must sit beside the video, the agenda and the registration list in one place.
  • More than one person has to moderate, or the moderators need roles.
  • You need to know who each attendee is, or to keep past events’ questions in an account.
  • The audience is larger than the launch-trial cap of 1,000 phones.
  • You need a named support contact and a service promise on the day.

Frequently asked questions

Are the questions anonymous?

People join with a nickname, not their real name. Untick “Show nicknames on the big screen” and the board shows the questions alone; your own page still shows the nickname so you can moderate.

Can a rude question reach the screen?

Not by default. Approval is on: a question waits on your page and reaches the board and the phones only when you press Approve. “Mask rude words” is also ticked by default. You can untick either one.

How many people can ask questions?

During the launch trial a free session takes up to 1,000 phones. When it is full, the next phone is told the session is full.

What happens to the questions?

Answers pass through our server only to reach the presenter’s screen. They are held in memory for the room’s life and deleted when it closes (24 hours at most). No accounts, no cookies, no analytics for participants.

Does it work without internet?

No. Questions travel from the phones to the presenter’s screen through our relay, so the presenter’s laptop and every phone need a connection. A room is deleted when it closes, and at the latest after 24 hours.

Can I vote twice for a question?

No. There is one upvote per phone per question, and tapping the arrow again takes it back. Answered questions cannot be voted on.
