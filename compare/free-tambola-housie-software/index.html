10 free tools linked from this page · none of them need an account

A tambola game needs three things: tickets, a caller, and a way to settle a claim. The tools here do all three. The ticket generator makes tickets in strips of six that use every number 1 to 90 once; the caller draws each number with your browser’s cryptographic random generator and speaks it; and Check a claim rebuilds a ticket from its printed number and tells you whether the prize is won. The same tickets work for housie and for 90-ball bingo.

What paid tambola and bingo apps do that we do not

These are commonly the reasons a club, a venue or a fundraiser pays for an app rather than uses a free tool.

  • It plays on the players’ phones. Many game apps give each player a digital ticket that marks itself as numbers are called. Here no phones join the game: players hold a printed ticket or a ticket picture and mark it themselves.
  • It sells tickets and takes payment. Ticket sales, payment and prize handling are common in commercial apps. Nothing here sells anything or holds money.
  • It runs a game for strangers online. Public rooms, matchmaking and hosted games are an app’s business. Here you host for people you already know, over a video call or in a room.
  • It claims for the player. Apps often let a player press a button to claim and settle it automatically. Here a player shouts their ticket number and the host types it into Check a claim.
  • It keeps a history. Past games, winners and player lists in an account are common. Here the game is kept on the host’s device until a new game starts, and there are no accounts.

When you should pay for a tambola or bingo app

If you are running a business or a charity event that sells tickets, a paid app is probably the right answer.

  • You sell tickets or take payment, and need the app to hold that for you.
  • You want each player’s ticket to live on their phone and mark itself.
  • You host games for people you do not know, online, on a regular schedule.
  • You need claims settled automatically and a history of winners.
  • The game is a regulated activity where you are, and your licence needs records an app keeps for you. We cannot advise on that; the regulator can.

Frequently asked questions

Are these real tambola tickets?

Yes. Each ticket has three rows and nine columns with 15 numbers, five in every row; the first column holds 1–9, the next 10–19 and so on, with 80–90 in the last; every column has one to three numbers, sorted top to bottom; and each strip of six uses every number 1 to 90 once. Our tests check 10,000 strips against those rules.

How do we play tambola on a video call?

Make the tickets with the Tambola Ticket Generator and send each player their ticket picture. Open the Tambola Number Caller with the same set code, press Show on TV and share that window in the call. Players mark their own tickets and shout their ticket number when they win; check it in the caller.

Is the number draw really random?

Every number is drawn from those not yet called using your browser’s cryptographic random number generator with rejection sampling, so each remaining number is equally likely. Our tests check the first draw of 9,000 games against an even spread with a chi-square test.

Is tambola the same as housie and 90-ball bingo?

The tickets are: three rows, nine columns, fifteen numbers, six to a strip. Only the prize names differ, and the caller can check One line and Two lines as well as the tambola prizes.

Does it need the internet?

No. After the first visit the pages open offline, and nothing about the game is sent to our server. Voices built into the device keep working offline; online voices need a connection.

Can people join on their phones?

Not to play the game. The tickets are printed or sent as pictures and marked by hand; the live phone tools on this site are for polls, quizzes and questions.
