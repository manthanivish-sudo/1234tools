Extract Audio from Video

Save the sound of a video as its own file in your browser: the original sound copied out untouched, or WAV, or a small Opus file. The whole sound or a part. Nothing is uploaded.

  • Free
  • Nothing uploaded
  • Untouched copy where it can

Why people use it

For podcasters, students and anyone who wants the sound of a video

Recorded lectures, calls and performances are stuck inside video files that music players will not open.

The usual way

  • ✗Playing the video and recording it again
  • ✗Uploading the video to an extractor site
  • ✗Installing an audio editor

With this tool

✓The original sound copied out untouched, or as WAV, or as a small Opus file.

Extract the sound ↑

What is extracting a video’s sound?

The sound in a video is a separate track, usually AAC in an MP4 or MOV and Opus in a WebM. Extracting it means writing that track into a file of its own that music players, editors and transcription tools open directly.

When the sound is copied out rather than decoded, the new file holds exactly the bytes that were in the video, so there is no loss and nothing to wait for.

How it works in your browser

The page lists only the formats it can honestly make from this file’s sound, with the untouched copy first when there is one.

  1. AAC is copied into an M4A container by mp4-muxer; Opus is copied into Ogg pages written by the page itself, with each page’s checksum and sample count.
  2. For WAV the sound is decoded with AudioDecoder and written as 16-bit samples at its own rate.
  3. For Opus the decoded sound is resampled to 48 kHz and encoded with AudioEncoder.
  4. From and To cut a WAV or Opus file to the sample: 2 s to 4 s of 48 kHz sound is exactly 96,000 samples.

Another worked example

From the 6-second test clip (AAC, 48 kHz, stereo), the untouched copy gave clip.m4a at 96.0 KB; WAV gave clip.wav at 1.10 MB; Opus at 64 kbit/s gave clip.ogg at 48.2 KB. All three decode to the clip’s own six notes in the right order.

Common uses

  • Lecture recordings Listen to a recorded class on the way home without the screen on.
  • Sampling your own footage Lift a sound effect or a line of dialogue for an edit.
  • Meeting minutes Hand the sound of a recorded meeting to whoever writes the notes.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing WAV to make a file smaller. WAV is uncompressed and far larger than the sound inside the video.
  • Re-encoding sound that could be copied. Every encode loses a little; the untouched copy loses nothing.

More questions

Which format should I choose for a podcast?

The untouched copy if it is offered, or Opus at 64 kbit/s for speech. Both are a small fraction of a WAV’s size.

Does the M4A keep the same quality as the video?

Yes, exactly: the AAC packets are copied byte for byte.

Can I take the sound from several videos?

One video at a time, each saved as its own file.

Privacy

Nothing you add is uploaded. The page reads your video from your device, takes its sound out in this tab and saves it to your downloads. The format and the Opus bitrate are remembered in this browser; the video, its name and the times are not kept. There is no account.

How it works

  1. Choose a video: MP4, MOV, MKV or WebM.
  2. Choose how to save the sound. Original sound, untouched copies it out as it is stored (AAC as an .m4a, Opus as an .ogg, MP3 as an .mp3). WAV is uncompressed. Opus is small, at 64, 96 or 128 kbit/s.
  3. For a part, type From and To in seconds; leave them for the whole sound.
  4. Press Extract the sound, then listen to it on the page or download it.

What people use it for

  • Podcasts and interviews — Take the conversation out of a video call recording for editing or listening on the go.
  • Music from your own videos — Keep the sound of a performance or a rehearsal you filmed.
  • Transcription — A WAV or Opus file is what most transcription tools ask for.
  • Voice notes — Turn a selfie video into a voice note you can send as a sound file.

Tips

  • Original sound, untouched is the best choice when it is offered: no loss, and it takes seconds.
  • WAV is about 10 MB a minute at 44.1 kHz stereo; Opus at 96 kbit/s is about 0.7 MB a minute.
  • For speech, Opus at 64 kbit/s is clear and very small.
  • A part copied untouched starts and ends on whole packets, within about 0.03 s of the times typed; WAV and Opus cut to the sample.

Limits and browser support

Files up to 2 GB; phones may run short of memory before that.

There is no MP3 encoder here: the usual one for the web (LAME) is under the LGPL, which this site does not ship. An MP3 sound track inside a video is copied out as an MP3.

Re-encoding to Opus or M4A needs WebCodecs’ audio encoder; where a browser lacks it, WAV and the untouched copy still work.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get the audio from an MP4?

Choose the MP4 and press Extract the sound. With Original sound, untouched, the AAC sound inside most MP4s is copied out as an .m4a file with no re-encoding, which plays on phones, computers and in music apps.

Can I save it as MP3?

Only when the video’s own sound is MP3, which is copied out as it is. The usual MP3 encoder for the web is under a licence this site does not ship, so there is no MP3 encoding; M4A, Opus and WAV play everywhere MP3 does.

Does it lose quality?

Not with the untouched copy or WAV. Opus and M4A re-encode the sound, which is very hard to hear at 96 kbit/s and above.

Can I take just one part?

Yes. Type From and To in seconds before extracting.

Why is the WAV so big?

WAV stores every sample uncompressed: 44,100 or 48,000 a second for each channel. It is the easiest format to edit and plays everywhere; choose Opus for a small file.

Is my video uploaded?

No. The page reads the file from your device and takes the sound out in this tab.
