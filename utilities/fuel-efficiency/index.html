What is fuel efficiency?

Fuel efficiency is how far a vehicle travels on a set amount of fuel, or how much fuel it burns over a set distance. The UK quotes miles per imperial gallon although pumps sell litres, the US uses miles per US gallon, and most of Europe uses litres per 100 km, so one car carries three different-looking figures.

For a trip budget, the number that matters is the fuel burned over the distance, and the cost is built on that.

How it is calculated

Every efficiency is converted to litres per 100 km first. The distance becomes kilometres, the fuel needed is the rate times the distance, and the cost is that volume at the pump price, turned into a price per litre if it was entered per US gallon.

Another worked example

A hire car’s trip computer shows 5.8 L/100 km, the round trip is 350 miles and fuel is £1.52 a litre. Choose L/100 km, Miles and Litre: the car needs 32.67 litres and the trip costs £49.66, or £0.14 a mile. On a British dashboard the same car would read 48.7 MPG; on an American one, 40.6 MPG.

Common uses

  • Comparing cars Put a UK brochure’s MPG and a European L/100 km figure on one scale before buying or hiring.
  • Budgeting a road trip Price a holiday drive at the fuel price where you will fill up.
  • Checking the trip computer Fill the tank twice, note the litres and miles between fills, and compare with the dashboard.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Entering imperial MPG as US MPG. 40 MPG from a UK brochure is 7.06 L/100 km; read as US gallons it becomes 5.88, and the fuel estimate falls by about 17%.
  • Leaving Price Per on US Gallon. That is the default, and a UK price per litre entered against it is spread over 3.785 litres, so the cost comes out far too low.

More questions

How do I convert UK MPG to US MPG?

Multiply by about 0.833, the ratio of the two gallons. 50 MPG in imperial gallons is 41.6 MPG in US gallons.

How much does it cost to drive 100 miles?

At 40 MPG (UK) and £1.50 a litre, 100 miles uses 11.37 litres and costs £17.05.

How do I work out km per litre?

Divide 100 by the L/100 km figure, or read the km/L result: 5.8 L/100 km is 17.24 km per litre.

Why is my real consumption worse than the official figure?

Official figures come from a standard test cycle. Short trips from cold, motorway speeds, roof boxes and air conditioning all raise real use.

Formula

Tips

  • MPG and L/100km are inverse measures: higher MPG is better, lower L/100km is better.
  • A US gallon is about 3.785 L; an Imperial gallon is about 4.546 L, so UK MPG figures look ~20% better than US figures for the same car.
  • Improving from 15 to 20 MPG saves more fuel per mile than improving from 40 to 50 MPG — the inverse relationship is counterintuitive.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the constant 235.214583 come from?

It is 100 × 3.785411784 (litres per US gallon) ÷ 1.609344 (km per mile), the factor that converts miles-per-gallon into litres-per-100-kilometres.
