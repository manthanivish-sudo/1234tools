Category Quiz Board

A quiz board of categories and points for the TV or the classroom projector: pick a square, reveal the answer, give the points to a team. One hidden double square. Build, save and print boards. Free, nothing uploaded.

What is a category quiz board?

A board of categories and points turns a quiz into a choice: which topic, how hard, how much to risk. It suits a classroom review because the class decides the order and the questions come from the unit.

The board here is a grid of squares worth 100 points in the top row, rising by 100 for each row.

How it works in your browser

The board is a grid of categories across and rows down, and each square holds a question, an answer and a state.

  1. A square’s points are 100 times its row, counting from the top as 1.
  2. When you start the game, one square is picked as the double with a secure random draw, each square equally likely.
  3. Right gives the square’s points to a team, doubled on the double; Wrong, if switched on, takes off the same amount.
  4. A used square greys out, and when none remain the TV shows the final scores.

Another worked example

The row values on the page came out as 100, 200, 300, 400, 500 and 600 for rows 1 to 6. On a five by five board there are 25 squares, and across 25,000 draws every square was chosen between 945 and 1,074 times, close to the 1,000 each that equal chances give. The last square of the grid sits in column 5 and row 5.

Common uses

  • Review before a test Five topics from the unit, played by teams for a point of pride.
  • Family games night Mix categories for every age and let the youngest pick first.
  • Office social A quick board on the projector to open a meeting.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Putting the hard questions at the top. The top row is worth the least, so keep it easy.
  • Shrinking the board and expecting the hidden squares to be lost. The text in them is kept until you enlarge the board again.

More questions

Can the host see which square is the double?

No. It is drawn at the start and stays hidden even from the host until a team picks it.

What does a wrong answer cost?

Nothing, unless you tick the box before you start. Then Wrong takes the square’s value off, and twice that on the double.

What does the printout contain?

Yes. You can print the board for a whiteboard version and a host’s sheet with every question and answer.

Privacy

Your boards and the scores stay on this device: they are kept in this browser’s own storage and shown on your own screens. Nothing is uploaded.

How it works

  1. Type a title for the board.
  2. Choose how many categories (across) and rows (down) the board has: 3 to 6 of each.
  3. Type a name for each category, then a question and its answer in each square. The page saves as you type.
  4. Press 2 Play, type the team names (one per line) and press Start the game.
  5. Press Show on TV and drag the new window to the TV or projector, then press Full screen there. With one screen, press Full screen here.
  6. Press a square on your page. The TV shows its question; your page also shows the answer.
  7. Press Reveal (or the R key) to show the answer on the TV.
  8. Press Right for the team that got it (or the team’s number key). Press it again to undo.
  9. Press Back to board (or B). The square greys out. When every square is used, the TV shows the final scores.
  10. To play again, press End game, then Start the game. A new double square is drawn.

Where people use it

  • Classroom review — five topics from the unit, easy to hard, played in teams before a test
  • Family games night — categories for every age, with the youngest choosing first
  • Club or office quiz — a quick board on the projector to start a meeting or a social

Tips

  • Put the easy questions at the top: the rows are worth 100, 200, 300 and so on going down.
  • The double square is drawn when you press Start the game, every square equally likely, and nobody sees which it is until it is picked — not even you.
  • Tick “Take points off for a wrong answer” for a harder game: each team then gets a Wrong button as well as Right.
  • Number keys 1 to 9 give the points to team 1 to 9, R reveals, B goes back to the board and S shows the scores.
  • Print the board for a whiteboard version, and the host’s sheet with every question and answer.
  • Changing the number of rows or categories keeps what you typed in the squares you hide, so you can change it back.

Frequently asked questions

Is anything uploaded?

No. The board, the questions and the scores are kept in this browser’s storage on this device and shown on your own screens. Export file saves a copy wherever you choose.

How is the double square chosen?

When you press Start the game, one square is drawn using your browser’s cryptographic random numbers, with every square equally likely. It stays hidden until a team picks it; then the TV says Double points and the square scores twice.

How big can the board be?

From 3 to 6 categories across and 3 to 6 rows down, so 9 to 36 squares. The rows are worth 100, 200, 300, 400, 500 and 600 points.

Can I take points off for a wrong answer?

Yes. Tick “Take points off for a wrong answer” before you start, and each team gets a Wrong button that takes off the square’s value (twice that on the double).

How do I show it on the classroom projector?

On the Play tab, press Show on TV, drag the new window to the projector and press Full screen there. Your laptop keeps the host’s view with the answers. With one screen, press Full screen here.

Can I reuse a board next year?

Yes. Boards are saved on this device under My boards; Export file saves a .json file you can keep or send, and Import file opens it on another device.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After the first visit the page opens without a connection, and your boards are kept on the device.
