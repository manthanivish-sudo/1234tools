What is an antakshari game?

Antakshari is a singing game: one team sings a song, and the next sings one that starts with the last sound of the first. The arguments are usually about the letter, the time and the score, not the songs.

The page deals with those three and leaves the singing to you. It holds no music, lyrics or song titles.

How it works in your browser

The page picks the starting letter and keeps the clock and the scores. The team supplies everything else.

  1. The letter is drawn with equal chance from the letters in play, using the secure random generator.
  2. You may leave out letters. The ones that remain are still equally likely.
  3. After a song, you tap or type its last sound and the next team receives it.
  4. Each round carries a rule that is shown on the TV; the game displays it but does not enforce it.

Another worked example

The Hindi set held 30 consonants, or 40 with the vowels included. The English set held 26 letters, and 23 after X, Q and Z were left out. Choosing both alphabets with vowels gave 66 letters in play. The game allows 2 to 4 teams and up to 8 rounds.

Common uses

  • Family party Two teams, forty-five seconds a turn and a rule for each decade.
  • Wedding evening Put the letter and clock on the projector for tables of guests.
  • Journey Play on one phone with no signal.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Expecting the page to check the song. It cannot; the host decides whether a song counts.
  • Choosing a rule so strict that nobody can answer. Start with any song.

More questions

Why are some Hindi letters missing?

The letters ङ, ञ and ण are left out because words seldom begin with them, and the vowels join only if you tick the box.

Can I redraw the letter?

Yes, before the clock starts. It is a fresh equal-chance draw, so use it only when the host agrees.

Does it need a connection?

No. After the first visit it opens offline, and a game in progress is saved.

Privacy

The team names and scores stay on this device. The page plays no music and sends nothing anywhere; a game in progress is saved on this device so a refresh can carry on.

How it works

  1. Choose how many teams (2 to 4), how long each turn lasts and how many points a song scores.
  2. Choose the letters in play, Hindi, English or both, and set a rule for each round, such as any song or songs from one decade.
  3. Press Start game. The first team and its letter appear.
  4. Press Show on TV for the big letter, the clock and the scores.
  5. Press Start timer (or N). The team sings a song that begins with the letter, from memory; the page plays no music.
  6. If the song was sung, press Song sung (or S). If the team cannot, wait for the clock or press Pass (or P).
  7. Tap the last sound of the song, or type it, and press Next team (or N). If you choose no letter, the next team gets a random one.
  8. After the last turn the winner is shown. Press Play again to start over.

Where people use it

  • Family evening — two teams, 45 seconds a turn and a round for each decade the grandparents love
  • Wedding or kitty party — four tables as teams, the letter and the clock on the projector
  • Road trip or school trip — two teams on one phone, no screen to share and no signal needed

Tips

  • The random letter is equally likely to be any letter in play. It is not weighted towards the letters songs usually start with, so a hard letter can come up just as often as an easy one.
  • If a letter is awkward, type it into “Leave out these letters” (for English you might leave out X, Q and Z). The letters that remain are still equally likely.
  • The page holds no songs, lyrics or titles and plays nothing. The team sings from memory and the host decides whether the song counts.
  • Rules are just the words you give each round, such as “Songs from one decade: 1990s”. They are shown on the TV; the game does not check them.
  • The clock is read against the device’s own time, so a slow or hidden tab still ends the turn on time.
  • Press New random letter (R) before the clock starts if the team and the host agree the letter is unfair. It is a fresh equal-chance draw each time.

Frequently asked questions

Does it play the songs?

No. There is no music, no lyrics and no list of song titles on the page. The game is only the letter, the clock and the score; your teams sing from memory.

How is the random letter chosen?

With equal chance from the letters in play, using your browser’s cryptographic random number generator. It is not weighted towards letters that songs usually start with.

Which letters are there?

Hindi: the 30 consonants from क to ह that words commonly start with (ङ, ञ and ण are left out), plus the 10 vowels if you tick Include vowels. English: A to Z. You can also choose both, and you can type letters to leave out.

How does the next letter work?

In antakshari the next team starts with the last sound of the previous song. After a song you tap that sound in the letter grid, or type it, and the next team gets it. If you choose none, the next team gets a random letter, whether or not the last team ran out of time.

Can I set my own rules?

Yes. Each round has a rule you choose: any song, songs from one decade, a film star, a singer, a theme or your own words. The rule is shown on the TV so everyone knows it; the host judges the songs.

How many teams can play?

Two to four, for up to 8 rounds. Each team has one to five turns in every round.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After the first visit it opens without a connection, and a game in progress is saved on this device so a refresh does not lose the scores.
