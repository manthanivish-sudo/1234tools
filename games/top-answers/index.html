Top Answers Survey Game

A family survey game for the TV: hidden answers with points, reveal them as teams guess, three strikes and the other team can steal. Type your own questions. Free, no sign-up, nothing uploaded.

For families, party hosts and teachers who like a survey game

A survey game needs a board, a scorekeeper and a buzzer for wrong guesses, and none of it is on hand.

What is a survey game?

In a survey game the right answer is the popular one. A question such as “Name something you take to the beach” has a handful of common answers, and teams score by guessing what a crowd would say.

The page holds the board, the strikes and the steal, and keeps the points as the answers are found.

How it works in your browser

Each question holds four to eight answers with points, hidden on the TV until a team finds them.

  1. Pressing an answer reveals it and adds its points to the bank for the round.
  2. A wrong guess is a strike. Three strikes end the playing team’s turn.
  3. Then the other team gets one guess. If it is on the board, that team takes the whole bank plus that answer; if not, the first team keeps the bank.
  4. The playing team swaps each question, and the scores carry to the final screen.

Another worked example

Using the beach question with six answers, team A found Towel for 30 points. Two strikes later the game was still in play, and the third strike moved it to the steal, which is three strikes in all. If the other team missed, A kept 30 and B none. When B instead found Swimsuit for 18, the bank came to 48 and all of it went to B.

Common uses

  • Kids against grown-ups Ask questions every age can answer.
  • Office social Survey colleagues beforehand and play back the answers.
  • Classroom Ask the class “name a fruit”, then play the result back as a game.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Writing a question with one right answer. A survey needs many plausible answers.
  • Inventing the points and presenting them as a real survey. The example points are made up; use your own count.

More questions

Can I add another spelling?

Yes. Put it under Also accept and a typed guess of it counts.

What if nobody gets the steal?

The playing team keeps the bank. The round ends and the unfound answers can be revealed.

How many questions can a game hold?

As many as you add. The game shows the final scores after the last one.

Privacy

Your questions, answers and the scores stay on this device: they are kept in this browser’s own storage and shown on your own screens. Nothing is uploaded.

How it works

  1. Type a title, then a question that asks for an answer many people would give, like “Name something you take to the beach”.
  2. Type 4 to 8 answers with their points, the most popular first. Press Sort by points if they are out of order.
  3. Press Add question for the next one. The page saves as you type.
  4. Press 2 Play, type the two team names and press Start the game.
  5. Press Show on TV and drag the new window to the TV, then press Full screen there. With one screen, press Full screen here.
  6. When the playing team names an answer on the board, press that answer (or its number key, 1 to 8). Or type their guess and press Check.
  7. When the guess is not on the board, press Strike (or the X key). The TV shows a big X and your device buzzes.
  8. After three strikes the other team has one guess to steal. Press the answer if they find one, or Steal missed.
  9. Press Reveal the rest to show the answers nobody found, then Next question (→).
  10. After the last question the TV shows the final scores. Press End game, then Start the game, to play again.

Where people use it

  • Family get-together — kids against grown-ups, with questions everyone can answer
  • Party or office social — two teams, a few questions surveyed among the guests beforehand
  • Classroom — a survey of the class (“name a fruit”) played back as a game the next day

Tips

  • The best questions have many possible answers: “Name something you find in a kitchen”, not “What is the capital of France?”.
  • For real points, ask the question to 20 to 100 people first (Print has a survey sheet) and count how many gave each answer.
  • A typed guess matches the answer or any “Also accept” spelling, ignoring capitals, accents, “the” and “a”, and one slip in a word of five letters or more.
  • If the stealing team names a hidden answer, they take every point on the board plus that answer; if not, the playing team keeps the board.
  • Number keys 1 to 8 reveal answers, X is a strike, U undoes, S shows the scores and → goes to the next question.
  • The playing team starts with team 1 and swaps every question; press Switch team before the first guess to change it.

Frequently asked questions

Is anything uploaded?

No. Your questions and the scores are kept in this browser’s storage on this device and shown on your own screens.

Where do the points come from?

From you. The example question has made-up points; change them to your own guesses, or ask a group of people first and use how many gave each answer. Print has a survey sheet for that.

How does stealing work?

After three strikes the other team talks it over and gives one answer. If it is on the board, they win every point revealed so far plus that answer. If not, the team that was playing wins the points on the board.

How many answers can a question have?

4 to 8, each with its points, most popular first. The TV shows them numbered, hidden until they are found.

Can more than two teams play?

The game is made for two teams, because the steal goes to the other team. For more teams, take turns in pairs or keep a running total in the Scoreboard.

How do I show it on the TV?

On the Play tab, press Show on TV, drag the new window to the TV or projector (or cast that tab) and press Full screen there. Your page keeps the answers. With one screen, press Full screen here.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After the first visit the page opens without a connection, and your questions are kept on the device.
