Silence Remover

Take the long pauses out of a recording in your browser: set how quiet and how long a silence is, see what will go on the waveform, keep a little either side so words are not clipped. Nothing is uploaded.

For podcasters, lecturers and voice-over artists

Long recordings are full of thinking time, and cutting each pause by hand takes longer than the recording.

The usual way

  • ✗Cutting every pause by hand
  • ✗Speeding the whole thing up instead
  • ✗Leaving the pauses in

Remove the silences ↑

What is removing silence?

Silence in a recording is rarely digital zero; it is room noise, breathing and hum at a low level. Removing it means choosing a level below which sound counts as silence, and a length below which a pause is part of the speech and should stay.

Cut too hard and speech sounds breathless, words run into each other and their first sounds are clipped; cut too gently and nothing changes. A little of each gap is worth keeping.

How it works in your browser

In a worker, the level of every 10 ms of sound is measured, and runs of quiet are collected.

  1. A window is quiet when the root-mean-square level of its loudest channel is below the threshold.
  2. Only runs of quiet windows at least the minimum length count as silences.
  3. From each silence the padding is kept at both ends, plus the pause you asked to leave; the rest is removed.
  4. The kept pieces are joined with 10 ms fades, and the waveform shades what goes before anything is saved.

Another worked example

A 4.90 s test recording with a 1.5 s and a 0.4 s silence, at −40 dBFS and 0.7 s, gave “1 silence found; 1.20 s will go”: the result lasted 3.70 s, the short pause untouched. Leaving a 0.5 s pause took out 0.70 s instead.

Common uses

  • Presentation rehearsals Hear your talk without the hesitations and see how long it really runs.
  • Phone call recordings Remove the waiting on hold.
  • Training data Trim the dead air from voice samples before using them.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Setting the threshold above the speaker’s quiet words. Watch the shading: if it covers speech, lower the threshold.
  • Removing every pause from a story. Leave a 0.25 s pause so sentences still breathe.

More questions

What threshold should I use?

Begin at −40 dBFS. A noisy room may need −35 or −30; a studio recording can go down to −50 or −60.

Does it work on videos?

It takes the sound of a video and saves a sound file; the picture is not edited.

Why keep a little either side?

Words fade in and out below the threshold. The padding keeps their first and last sounds from being cut.

Privacy

Nothing you add is uploaded. Your file is decoded by your browser’s own audio decoder, and the silences are found and cut in a worker in this tab. The settings are remembered in this browser; the file and its name are not kept. There is no account.

How it works

  1. Choose a recording or a video.
  2. Set how quiet counts as silence (−30 to −60 dBFS) and how long it must last (0.3 to 3 s). The silences found are shaded on the waveform, with how much will go.
  3. Choose how much to keep either side so words are not clipped, and whether to leave nothing or a short pause in each gap.
  4. Choose WAV, M4A or Opus and press Remove the silences.

What people use it for

  • Podcasts and interviews — Take out the long thinking pauses without editing them one by one.
  • Lecture recordings — Remove the minutes of quiet while the class works.
  • Voice-overs — Tighten a read-through so it fits the video.
  • Dictation — Make a long voice memo quicker to listen back to.

Tips

  • Start at −40 dBFS and 0.7 s; raise the threshold towards −30 if the room was noisy, lower it towards −60 for a quiet studio.
  • Leaving a 0.25 s pause in each gap keeps speech natural; cutting it all makes a tight, quick edit.
  • Each join gets a 10 ms fade so it does not click.
  • Nothing is removed until you press the button: change the settings and watch the shading move.

Limits and browser support

Files up to 400 MB and 3 hours; on a phone keep files short.

Silence is judged by level only: steady background noise louder than the threshold counts as sound.

M4A and Opus need WebCodecs’ audio encoder; where a browser lacks it, WAV still works.

Frequently asked questions

How does it decide what is silence?

It measures the level of the loudest channel over every 10 ms. A stretch where that stays under the threshold for at least the minimum length counts as one silence.

Will it cut the ends of my words?

Not with the default setting: 0.15 s of each silence is kept either side, so breaths and the tails of words stay.

Can I keep short pauses?

Yes. Silences shorter than the minimum length are left alone, and Leave in its place can shorten long ones to a 0.25 or 0.5 s pause instead of removing them.

Can I see what will be removed first?

Yes: the waveform shades every stretch that will go, and the page says how many seconds that comes to.

Does it work on music?

It finds silences anywhere, but quiet passages in music can fall under the threshold; check the shading before you save.

Is my file uploaded?

No. Everything happens in this tab.
