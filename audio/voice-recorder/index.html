Voice Recorder

Record your voice in the browser with a level meter, a clock and pause, then save the recording as it is or as WAV, M4A or Opus. Choose the microphone and switch noise suppression off for music. Nothing is uploaded.

  • Free
  • Nothing uploaded

Why people use it

For anyone who needs a quick recording on a computer

Phone recorders are fiddly to get off the phone, and online recorders upload what you say.

What is recording in the browser?

Browsers can record a microphone without any plug-in: the page asks for permission, the browser shows that the microphone is on, and the MediaRecorder interface compresses the sound as it arrives.

The format is the browser’s choice. Chrome, Edge and Firefox record Opus in a WebM file; Safari records AAC in an MP4. Both are small and good for speech, and either can be converted afterwards.

How it works in your browser

The page asks for the microphone with the browser’s own echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain, unless you switch them off.

  1. MediaRecorder is asked for 64 kbit/s and hands the page a piece of the recording every second.
  2. The level bar shows the peak of the last 2,048 samples, redrawn about sixty times a second.
  3. Pause and Resume stop and restart the recording and the clock; the microphone stays open while paused.
  4. Stop closes the microphone and puts the pieces together into one file; Save as decodes it and writes WAV, M4A or Opus.

Another worked example

In Chrome with its test microphone, a recording paused once and stopped after 3.22 s decoded to 3.18 s of WebM sound at 56 kbit/s; saved as WAV it became recording-1.wav at 48 kHz.

Common uses

  • Feedback for students Record spoken comments on a piece of work.
  • Audition tapes A quick recording of a monologue or a song.
  • Meeting reminders A spoken list of actions while they are fresh.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Closing the tab before downloading. The recording lives only in the page.
  • Recording music with noise suppression on: it treats held notes as noise.

More questions

Does it work on a phone?

Yes, in the phone’s browser, with the same permission prompt. Keep the screen on: some phones pause a page that goes to the background.

Can I record the computer’s sound as well?

Not here: the page records a microphone. Recording a screen with its sound is a separate tool.

Is the recording saved anywhere?

Only in the page’s memory until you download it.

Privacy

Nothing you record is uploaded. The microphone is read by your browser and the recording is kept in this tab until you save it or close the page; the browser shows its own recording indicator while the microphone is on. The processing switches and the save format are remembered in this browser; the recording and the microphone’s name are not kept. There is no account.

How it works

  1. Press Record and allow the browser to use your microphone. The meter shows the level and the clock counts.
  2. Pause and Resume as often as you like; Stop ends the recording and turns the microphone off.
  3. Download the recording as the browser made it, or press Save as to make a WAV, M4A or Opus copy.
  4. Once the browser has been allowed, the Microphone list shows every microphone it can use.

What people use it for

  • Voice notes and messages — Record a message to send as a sound file.
  • Practice — Record a speech, a song or an instrument and listen back.
  • Narration — Record a voice-over for slides or a video.
  • Interviews — Record a conversation on a laptop, then trim and normalise it here.

Tips

  • Keep the meter in the upper half while you speak, and away from the right-hand end, where it clips.
  • For music, untick echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain: they are made for speech and can pump or dull an instrument.
  • A headset or a microphone close to the mouth beats the laptop’s built-in one.
  • Recordings are kept in the page only: download before you close the tab.

Limits and browser support

The recording is held in the page’s memory: at the 64 kbit/s it asks for, an hour is about 30 MB in Chrome and Edge.

The format as recorded is the browser’s choice: Opus in WebM in Chrome, Edge and Firefox, AAC in MP4 in Safari.

The page needs permission to use the microphone, and a secure (https) address, which this site is.

Frequently asked questions

Is my recording sent anywhere?

No. The browser hands the microphone’s sound to this page, which keeps it in memory until you download it. Nothing is uploaded, and closing the tab discards it.

How long can I record?

As long as the device has memory for it: the page asks for 64 kbit/s, about 30 MB an hour. The clock shows how long you have been going.

Why does the browser ask for permission?

Every browser asks before a page may use the microphone, and shows a recording indicator while it is on. You can take the permission back in the address bar at any time.

Can I choose which microphone?

Yes. After the first recording, or once permission is given, the Microphone list shows every microphone the browser can use.

Why turn off noise suppression?

It is tuned for speech and treats a held note or a guitar’s sustain as noise to remove. For music, switch it and the other two off.

Can I save as MP3?

No, for licence reasons: WAV, M4A and Opus play everywhere MP3 does.
