Work out a percentage ↑

What is a percentage?

A percentage is a number of parts per hundred: 35% means 35 out of every 100, or 0.35 as a decimal. It puts amounts of different sizes on one scale, which is why a 6-mark gain means more on a 40-mark test than on a 120-mark one.

Almost every percentage question is one of three sums: a part of a whole, the change from an old value to a new one, or the amount before a percentage was added or taken away.

How it is calculated

Each result rearranges part = whole × rate ÷ 100 for the missing number. Percentage change divides by the starting value, so swapping A and B changes the answer.

A as a % of B = A ÷ B × 100% change from A to B = (B − A) ÷ A × 100B increased by A% = B × (1 + A ÷ 100)% difference = |A − B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2) × 100

Another worked example

A shop raises a price from £60 to £75. With 60 as Value A and 75 as Value B, the % change from A to B is 25%. Read the other way, 60 is 80% of 75, so going back from £75 to £60 would be a 20% cut: the same £15, measured against a larger starting point. The symmetric % difference, 22.222%, sits between the two because it measures the gap against the average, £67.50.

Common uses

  • Discounts and price rises Check whether “was £75, now £60” really is the 20% off the label claims.
  • Exam marks Turn 54 out of 72 into a percentage, or find how many marks 65% of a paper needs.
  • Pay rises and inflation Compare a rise in pounds with the percentage quoted, or with inflation.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Dividing by the wrong base. Percentage change always divides by the starting value: a rise from 50 to 60 is 20%, not 16.7%.
  • Undoing a rise by subtracting the same percentage. A £120 price that includes a 20% increase came from £100 (120 ÷ 1.2), not £96.

More questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100: 15% of 240 is 240 × 15 ÷ 100 = 36. Here, put 15 in Value A and 240 in Value B and read “A% of B”.

How do I work out the percentage increase between two numbers?

Divide the rise by the old value and multiply by 100: from 80 to 92 is 12 ÷ 80 × 100 = 15%. Enter the old value as A.

How do I find the original price before a discount?

Divide the sale price by one minus the discount as a decimal. A coat at £68 after 15% off was £68 ÷ 0.85 = £80; adding 15% back gives the wrong £78.20. Here, enter 15 and 68 and read “B before an A% cut”.

Can a percentage be more than 100%?

A share cannot, but a change can: sales rising from 40 to 100 is a 150% increase.

Tips

  • Percentage change is directional: going 100 → 50 is −50%, but 50 → 100 is +100%. The same absolute move gives different percentages.
  • A 20% drop followed by a 20% rise does not return you to the start — it leaves you 4% down.
  • Percentage difference (symmetric) compares two values without treating either as the baseline.
  • Choose a question to get a form for just that sum, with the working step by step under the formula.
  • To find a price before VAT or a discount, use the figure before a rise or cut: dividing is right, taking the percentage off the new figure is not.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between percentage points and percent?

If a rate moves from 5% to 7%, that is a rise of 2 percentage points, but a 40% increase in relative terms. Mixing the two is a common source of misleading statistics.

How do I turn a fraction into a percentage?

Divide the top by the bottom and multiply by 100: 3/8 is 0.375, which is 37.5%. Choose “Fraction, decimal and percentage” and type 3/8, 1 1/2, 0.375 or 37.5% to see all three forms and the simplest fraction.
