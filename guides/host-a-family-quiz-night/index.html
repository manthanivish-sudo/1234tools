How to host a family quiz night

Split the players into fair teams with the Random Team Generator, open the Quiz Buzzer so every team has a key or a touch pad, keep the points on the Quiz Scoreboard (the buzzer can send them there for you), and put both on the TV with Show on TV. Spin the Wheel picks the next category, a forfeit or a prize.

6 min read · 5 steps · 4 ways it goes wrong

What you need before you start

  • The questions: five rounds of eight to ten is plenty. Mix easy and hard so every age gets some right.
  • A laptop for the host, and a TV or projector if you have one. A keyboard or a touch screen for the buzzers.
  • The names of everyone playing, for the teams.

1. Make fair teams

Type or paste the names, one per line, and choose how many teams. For teams that are even in strength, add a number after a comma (“Asha, 4”) and choose Balanced by skill; to keep two people apart, add a line such as “Asha / Ravi”.

Press Make teams, then Reveal one by one on the TV for a little ceremony. Team sizes never differ by more than one.

The tool for this step: Random Team Generator — Free, No Sign-up — splits the names into fair random teams, balanced by skill or with people kept apart, and shows them on the TV.

2. Set up the buzzer

Choose the number of teams and name them. Each team gets a key on the keyboard and a pad on the screen; on a tablet several teams can press their pads at once. R, C and W are kept for the host.

Tick “Send points to the scoreboard” so that pressing Correct adds the points on the Quiz Scoreboard automatically.

The tool for this step: Quiz Buzzer — Free, No Sign-up — decides who pressed first from each press’s time stamp and locks the other teams out.

3. Open the scoreboard and put it on the TV

Open the scoreboard in another tab with the same teams. Press Show on TV: a big leaderboard that re-orders as the scores change. Use +1, +5 or −1 for a team, or the keys 1 to 9 to pick a team and + or − to change its score.

The tool for this step: Quiz Scoreboard — Free, No Sign-up — keeps every team’s points round by round, with a double-points joker per team, undo and a CSV of the results.

4. Run each round

Read the question and say go. The first team to press is shown; press Correct (C) to give the points or Wrong (W) to lock that team out and let the others try. Press Reset (R) for the next question, and Next round on the scoreboard when the round ends.

Between rounds, spin the wheel to choose the next category, a forfeit for the losing team or the order of play. Tick “Remove the winner after each spin” so nothing comes up twice.

The tool for this step: Spin the Wheel — Free, No Sign-up — picks fairly from a list on the TV, with the winner chosen by the browser’s random generator before the wheel moves.

5. Finish and keep the results

After the last round the scoreboard shows the final order; equal totals share a rank. Export the results as a CSV file if you keep a league table from week to week.

Where this usually goes wrong

4 things that actually happen, rather than a note asking you to be careful.

  • Two teams sharing a key. The buzzer refuses a key another team already has and keeps the old one. Give each team a key on opposite sides of the keyboard so hands do not collide.
  • Scoring in the wrong round. Points always go into the round shown in “Scoring round”. Press Next round when a round ends, and choose an earlier round from the list only to fix it.
  • Questions that only the adults can answer. Mixed-age teams make this fair: balance the teams by giving each person a rough strength from 1 to 5, and include a picture or kids’ round.
  • Playing a joker too late. A joker doubles a team’s points in the round it is played, minus points included. Ask teams to say which round they are playing it on before the round starts.

How long this should take

About twenty minutes to set up the first time, not counting the questions. Five rounds of ten questions take an hour to an hour and a half with a family audience.

Frequently asked questions

Do the players need phones?

No. The buzzer runs on one keyboard or touch screen, with a key or pad per team, and everything else is on the host’s laptop and the TV.

Can I run it without a TV?

Yes. Everything works on the laptop screen; Show on TV just gives the room a bigger view.

Does anything get uploaded?

No. Names, teams and scores stay in your browser on this device.

Can I use it in a classroom?

Yes. A projector and one keyboard are enough for four to six teams, and the team generator can keep particular pupils apart.
