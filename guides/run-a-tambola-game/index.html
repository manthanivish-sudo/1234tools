How to run a tambola (housie) game

Make the tickets in the Tambola Ticket Generator (in strips of six, printed or sent as pictures on WhatsApp), open the Tambola Number Caller and press Show on TV, call numbers with Next number or auto-call, and when someone claims, type the ticket number printed on their ticket into Check a claim.

6 min read · 5 steps · 5 ways it goes wrong

What you need before you start

  • A laptop or phone to call from. A TV or projector helps everyone see the board, but is not essential.
  • A printer and scissors for paper tickets, or the players’ WhatsApp numbers for ticket pictures. Pens or dabbers for marking.
  • The prizes you will play for, decided in advance: usually Early Five, the three lines, Four Corners and Full House.

1. Make the tickets

Tickets come in strips of six: a strip uses every number from 1 to 90 exactly once. Make one ticket per player plus a few spare. For 20 people, 24 tickets is four strips.

Type a title (the party’s name), pick a colour and press Print, Download PDF or Ticket pictures. Each ticket shows a set code and a ticket number such as K7MPQ2-014. Write the set code down: typing it again reprints exactly the same tickets.

The tool for this step: Tambola Ticket Generator — Free, No Sign-up — makes valid tambola tickets in strips of six, prints 6 or 12 to a page with cut lines, or makes one PNG picture per ticket to send on WhatsApp.

2. Hand out the tickets and agree the prizes

Give out paper tickets, or send each player their own ticket picture. Read out the prizes before the first number and how many claims each prize allows. A common house rule: a claim must be made before the next number is called.

  • Early Five: the first player to mark any five numbers.
  • Top, Middle and Bottom line: every number in that row.
  • Four Corners: the first and last numbers of the top and bottom rows.
  • Full House: all fifteen numbers on the ticket.

3. Put the caller on the TV

Open the caller on the laptop and press Show on TV. A second window opens with the number huge, its call, the 1–90 board, the last five numbers and the prizes won. Drag it to the TV or projector and make it full screen. On a video call, share that window instead.

Choose the voice: English with the Indian tambola calls, English with the British bingo calls, or the numbers in Hindi (this needs a Hindi voice on the device; the page says so if there is none).

The tool for this step: Tambola Number Caller — Free, No Sign-up — draws each number fairly from those not yet called, says it aloud and keeps the TV board up to date.

4. Call the numbers

Press Next number, or Space, for each call. For a steady pace press Start auto-call and choose 3 to 20 seconds between numbers; P pauses and resumes. If the voice is still speaking when the time is up, the next number waits for it.

Called one by mistake? Undo last call puts it back. If the page is refreshed or the phone sleeps, the game comes back as it was; only auto-call needs starting again.

5. Check every claim from the ticket number

When someone shouts, pause and ask for the ticket number printed on their ticket. Type it into Check a claim and pick the prize. The page rebuilds that exact ticket, marks the numbers called and says whether the claim is good or which numbers are still missing, and at which call the prize was complete.

A good claim can be awarded: it appears on the TV, so everyone sees who won what. Then carry on calling until Full House.

Where this usually goes wrong

5 things that actually happen, rather than a note asking you to be careful.

  • Printing too few tickets. Tickets only come in whole strips of six. Count the players, add a few spare for latecomers and people who want two, and round up to the next six.
  • Losing the set code. The set code on every ticket is what lets you reprint the same tickets and is part of each ticket number. Keep one spare ticket or a photo of it; without the code a lost ticket cannot be remade.
  • Accepting a claim without checking it. People mark numbers that were never called, or mishear one. Type the ticket number into Check a claim every time: it takes seconds and ends arguments, because everyone can see the result on the TV.
  • Expecting a Hindi voice that is not there. The Hindi calls need a Hindi text-to-speech voice on the calling device. Most Android phones have one; Windows, iPhone and Mac can add one in their language settings. Without it the caller speaks English, and says so.
  • Calling too fast for the room. Children and older players need time to find each number. Auto-call at 10 to 15 seconds suits most family games; pause with P while a claim is checked.

How long this should take

About fifteen minutes before the guests arrive the first time: most of it is printing and cutting the tickets. A game of 90 numbers takes 30 to 45 minutes at a relaxed pace, or about 20 with auto-call at 10 seconds.

Frequently asked questions

Is tambola the same as housie and 90-ball bingo?

Yes. They use the same ticket: three rows, nine columns and fifteen numbers, six tickets to a strip. Only the prize names differ, and the caller can also check One line and Two lines for 90-ball bingo.

Can we play tambola on a video call?

Yes. Send each player their ticket picture on WhatsApp, share the caller’s TV window in the call, and check claims by ticket number. Players mark their own tickets at home.

Do I need to note who has which ticket?

No. The ticket number is enough: the caller rebuilds the ticket from it and checks it against the numbers called.

Is anything sent to a server?

No. The tickets, the PDF, the pictures and the calling all happen in your browser, on your device.
