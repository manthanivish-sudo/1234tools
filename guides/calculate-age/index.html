How to calculate your age exactly

To calculate your exact age, subtract your date of birth from today’s date in three columns — years, months, days — borrowing the days of the previous month when the day column goes negative, exactly as in column subtraction. Someone born on 15 June 1990 is 36 years, 3 months and 19 days old on 4 October 2026, and has been alive for 13,260 days.

7 min read · 5 steps · 5 ways it goes wrong

What you need before you start

  • The date of birth, written unambiguously — day, month name and year — so that 03/04 cannot mean two different dates.
  • The date you want the age on: today, or the cut-off date an exam, scheme or form specifies.
  • For official purposes, the rule the organisation uses. Most count completed years, as below; a few count differently.

1. Write both dates as year, month, day

Line the dates up like a subtraction sum, with the later date on top.

2026-10-04 (the date you want the age on)

1990-06-15 (the date of birth), subtracted

2. Subtract the days, borrowing a month if you need to

Start from the right. 4 minus 15 does not go, so borrow one month and add the number of days in the month before the reference month. The month before October is September, which has 30 days: 4 + 30 − 15 = 19 days. Because a month was borrowed, the month column on top drops from 10 to 9.

3. Subtract the months, borrowing a year if you need to

Now 9 − 6 = 3 months. If the top number had been smaller, you would borrow a year — add 12 to the months and take one off the years. Here there is no need, so the years are simply 2026 − 1990 = 36.

The answer: 36 years, 3 months, 19 days. The same calculation gives a whole number of years if that is all you need: you are 36 because the birthday in June has already passed this year.

4. Work out an age on a cut-off date

Exams, school admissions, pension schemes and job adverts often set an age limit “as on” a particular date. Use that date as the reference, not today. Someone born on 20 November 2008 is 17 years, 10 months and 14 days old on 4 October 2026 — not yet 18, and will not be until 20 November 2026, 47 days later. If the cut-off were 1 December 2026, they would qualify.

5. Handle 29 February and the end of the month

Someone born on 29 February has a birthday on that date only in leap years. In other years the calculator treats the birthday as falling on 1 March: on 28 February 2027 a person born on 29 February 2004 is 22 years, 11 months and 30 days old, and turns 23 on 1 March. Rules for legal purposes differ between countries, so for anything official, check which date the organisation uses.

Month ends cause a similar wrinkle. From 31 January to 1 April is two months and one day, because the calendar walks from 31 January to 31 March and then one more day. Another calculator that counts months differently can be a day out on dates like these; neither is wrong, they follow different conventions.

Where this usually goes wrong

5 things that actually happen, rather than a note asking you to be careful.

  • Subtracting the years and stopping there. 2026 − 1990 is 36, but someone born in December 1990 is still 35 on 4 October 2026. The year subtraction is only right once the birthday has passed in the reference year. Check the months and days before you trust it.
  • Dividing the days by 365. Dividing a day count by 365 drifts by about a day every four years because of leap years, and dividing by 365.25 gives a decimal that is not how anyone states an age. Walk the calendar — years, months, days — as above.
  • Reading 03/04 the wrong way round. In India and the UK 03/04/2005 is 3 April; in the United States it is 4 March. A date of birth typed in the wrong order gives an age a month out, and the mistake survives because both dates are valid. Write the month as a name, or use year-month-day.
  • Counting both the first and the last day. The difference between two dates counts the days in between, so the day of birth itself is not an extra day lived. Counting inclusively — both ends — adds one, which matters when a rule says “at least 6,570 days”.
  • Using today when the form asks for a cut-off date. An age limit “as on 1 August 2026” is checked against 1 August, not the day you fill the form in. Someone eligible today can be ineligible on the cut-off, and the other way round.

How long this should take

A minute by hand, seconds in the calculator. The time worth spending is on the reference date: an application that asks for your age “as on 1 August” wants that date, not today’s.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my age in Excel?

=DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y") gives completed years. "YM" gives the months after the last birthday and "MD" the days, though Microsoft warns that "MD" can give wrong results at some month ends — check those against a calendar.

How many days old am I?

Subtract your date of birth from today and count the days, or let the calculator do it: someone born on 15 June 1990 is 13,260 days old on 4 October 2026.

How do I work out my age on a specific date?

Use that date in place of today. In the Age Calculator, change “Age at date” to the date you need and the exact age on that day appears.

Why do two age calculators give different answers by a day?

Usually because of month-end or leap-day births, where there is more than one reasonable way to count a month. Check which convention the organisation asking for your age uses.

Is my date of birth sent anywhere?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.
