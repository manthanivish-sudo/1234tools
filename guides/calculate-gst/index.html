How to calculate GST in India

To add GST, multiply the price by the rate: ₹25,000 at 18% carries ₹4,500 of GST, so the invoice total is ₹29,500. To take GST out of a price that already includes it, divide by one plus the rate — ₹11,800 ÷ 1.18 = ₹10,000 before tax, with ₹1,800 of GST — and never simply subtract 18%.

9 min read · 5 steps · 5 ways it goes wrong

What you need before you start

  • The amount, and whether it includes GST or not. A quotation is usually exclusive; a shelf price or a figure marked “inclusive of all taxes” already contains the tax.
  • The GST rate for that specific item or service, which comes from its HSN code (goods) or SAC code (services).
  • Where the supply is going — the place of supply. It decides whether the tax is split into CGST and SGST, or charged as a single IGST.

1. Find the rate for what you are selling

GST is charged at a rate set for each category of goods and services, identified by its HSN or SAC code. The everyday name of a product is not enough: two things that look alike can sit under different codes at different rates. Look the code up, then the rate.

The figures here, and when they were last checked

From 22 September 2025 the main GST rates are 5% and 18%, with a 40% rate on a short list of luxury and “sin” goods; the earlier 12% and 28% slabs were removed on the GST Council’s recommendation at its 56th meeting. Special rates remain for a few goods, such as 3% on gold and silver and 0.25% on rough diamonds, and some supplies are exempt or nil-rated. The rate for a particular item is fixed by the rate notifications CBIC publishes against its HSN or SAC code. Only a supplier registered for GST may charge it on an invoice. Whether a supply is taxed as CGST plus SGST or as IGST depends on its place of supply, under rules set out in the IGST Act.

Checked against the sources below on 4 October 2026. Rates, thresholds and deadlines move — at a Budget, at a notification, sometimes between one. Anything you are going to act on, check at the source.

  • GST Council — recommendations of the 56th meeting (press release, September 2025) — the 5%, 18% and 40% structure and the date it took effect
  • CBIC-GST — rates, notifications and the tariff — rate notifications by HSN and SAC code, and the Acts
  • Goods and Services Tax portal — registration and returns

2. Decide whether your figure includes GST

An exclusive figure is the taxable value: GST goes on top. An inclusive figure already has GST inside it: the job is to separate the two. Getting it backwards on ₹11,800 at 18% gives ₹13,924 (tax added twice) or ₹9,676 (tax taken off the wrong base) — both wrong.

3. Add GST to a price that does not include it

GST = taxable value × rate ÷ 100

total = taxable value + GST

For a ₹25,000 job at 18%: 25,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹4,500 of GST, so the invoice total is ₹29,500. Or in one step, 25,000 × 1.18 = 29,500. For goods sold by quantity, work out the taxable value first — 40 units at ₹450 is ₹18,000 — and at 5% that carries ₹900 of GST, a total of ₹18,900.

4. Take GST out of a price that already includes it

taxable value = inclusive price ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100)

GST = inclusive price − taxable value

For ₹11,800 including 18%: 11,800 ÷ 1.18 = ₹10,000, so the GST inside it is ₹1,800. For ₹1,050 including 5%: 1,050 ÷ 1.05 = ₹1,000, with ₹50 of tax. A useful check: the taxable value plus the GST must add back to exactly the price you started with.

For the tax alone: inclusive price × rate ÷ (100 + rate). 11,800 × 18 ÷ 118 = ₹1,800.

5. Split the tax into CGST and SGST, or IGST

A supply within one state is taxed half by the Centre and half by the state: 18% becomes 9% CGST plus 9% SGST (or UTGST in a union territory without a legislature). On the ₹25,000 job that is ₹2,250 CGST and ₹2,250 SGST. A supply from one state to another is taxed as a single IGST at the full rate: ₹4,500 IGST. The customer pays the same total either way.

What decides it is the place of supply — for goods, usually where they are delivered; for services, rules of their own — not where your office is.

Where this usually goes wrong

5 things that actually happen, rather than a note asking you to be careful.

  • Subtracting the rate to remove GST. Knocking 18% off ₹11,800 gives ₹9,676. The real taxable value is ₹10,000, because the 18% was charged on ₹10,000, not on ₹11,800. Divide by 1.18. On a single invoice the error is ₹324; across a year of inclusive receipts it misstates both the turnover and the tax.
  • Using a rate from before September 2025. The 12% and 28% slabs went on 22 September 2025, but old price lists, invoice templates and many web pages still carry them. Check the current notification for the code before you invoice.
  • Choosing IGST or CGST plus SGST by where you sit. The split follows the place of supply. A Delhi supplier delivering goods within Delhi charges CGST and SGST; deliver the same goods to Mumbai and it is IGST. Services, and goods delivered to someone other than the buyer, have rules of their own — look them up rather than going by the addresses on the letterhead. The wrong split means the customer cannot take the credit as charged, and the fix is a corrected invoice.
  • Rounding the halves differently. Round CGST up and SGST down and the halves no longer add to the total, so the invoice disagrees with itself. Halve the total GST and round both halves the same way.
  • Leaving charges out of the taxable value. Packing, delivery and similar charges billed on the same invoice usually form part of the value the tax is worked out on. Calculating GST on the goods alone and adding the delivery charge afterwards untaxed understates the tax, which is the kind of difference that appears when a customer reconciles their purchase register.

How long this should take

Under a minute per figure once you know the rate and whether your price includes tax. Finding the right rate for a product the first time can take longer than every calculation after it, because the rate follows the HSN or SAC code, not the everyday name of the thing.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate 18% GST on ₹1,000?

1,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹180 of GST, so the total is ₹1,180. Within one state that is ₹90 CGST and ₹90 SGST; to another state it is ₹180 IGST.

What is the formula to remove GST from a total?

Divide the total by one plus the rate. For 18%, divide by 1.18; for 5%, divide by 1.05. The answer is the taxable value, and the GST is the total minus that.

What is the difference between CGST, SGST and IGST?

They are the same tax shared differently. On a supply within a state, the Centre collects CGST and the state collects SGST, each at half the rate. On a supply between states, the Centre collects IGST at the full rate. The total the customer pays is the same.

Does the calculator know which rate applies to my product?

No. It offers the current slabs; the rate for your item comes from the CBIC notification for its HSN or SAC code, or from your accountant.

A price says “inclusive of all taxes”. How much of it is GST?

Treat it as an inclusive amount: divide by one plus the rate to find the taxable value, and the rest is GST. At 18%, a ₹590 item holds ₹500 of value and ₹90 of tax.
