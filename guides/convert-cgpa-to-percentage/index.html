How to convert CGPA to percentage

Multiply your CGPA by the factor your board or university publishes: for a CBSE class 10 CGPA it is 9.5, so 8.2 is 77.9%; many universities use a plain × 10, so 8.2 is 82%; and some subtract an offset first — VTU’s (CGPA − 0.75) × 10 turns 8.2 into 74.5%. Use the formula printed on your own marksheet or in your university’s regulations, because that is the only one an admissions office or employer will accept.

7 min read · 4 steps · 5 ways it goes wrong

What you need before you start

  • Your CGPA exactly as printed on the marksheet or transcript, to the decimal places shown.
  • Your board’s or university’s conversion rule. Look on the reverse of the grade card, in the examination ordinance, or on the university’s website.
  • The maximum of your scale — 10 for most Indian boards and universities, 4 for some institutions abroad.

1. Find the formula your institution uses

There is no single national CGPA-to-percentage formula. Each board or university sets its own, and the difference matters: on a CGPA of 8.2 the common formulas give answers more than seven percentage points apart. The rule is usually printed on the back of the grade card. If it is not there, it will be in the examination regulations.

The figures here, and when they were last checked

CBSE’s rule for the grades it introduced at secondary level from 2009–10 is an indicative percentage of 9.5 × CGPA overall, and 9.5 × the grade point for each subject. It was set out in Circular No. 24 of 28 May 2010 and applies to CBSE class 10 results reported as grades — not to a university degree CGPA.

Checked against the sources below on 4 October 2026. Rates, thresholds and deadlines move — at a Budget, at a notification, sometimes between one. Anything you are going to act on, check at the source.

  • CBSE Circular No. 24 of 28 May 2010 — grading at secondary level, and the indicative percentage — overall indicative percentage of marks = 9.5 × CGPA

2. Apply it to your CGPA

percentage = (CGPA − offset) × multiplier

Most formulas are this shape. A plain ten-point scale has no offset and multiplies by 10. CBSE has no offset and multiplies by 9.5. VTU subtracts 0.75, GTU subtracts 0.5, and both then multiply by 10.

3. Work a percentage requirement back into a CGPA

CGPA = percentage ÷ multiplier + offset

An admission or job advert asking for “75% or equivalent” needs the reverse. Under the ten-point rule, 75% is a CGPA of 7.5. Under CBSE’s rule it is 75 ÷ 9.5 = 7.89. Under GTU it is 75 ÷ 10 + 0.5 = 8.0, and under VTU 75 ÷ 10 + 0.75 = 8.25. The same percentage needs a CGPA up to 0.75 higher depending on where you studied.

4. If your scale is not out of 10, or you only have SGPAs

On a 4-point scale, set the maximum to 4 and use the multiplier your institution publishes; × 25 is a common one, which makes 3.4 into 85%. Do not assume it — many institutions on a 4-point scale publish no percentage equivalent at all, and say so.

If your marksheets give a grade point average for each semester (SGPA) and you need the overall CGPA, it is normally the credit-weighted average: multiply each semester’s SGPA by its credits, add them up, and divide by the total credits. Then convert that CGPA.

Where this usually goes wrong

5 things that actually happen, rather than a note asking you to be careful.

  • Using CBSE’s 9.5 for a university degree. The 9.5 rule is CBSE’s, for its own grades. It is widely repeated online as if it were a general rule, and a university that publishes × 10 or an offset formula will not accept a figure worked out with it. Use your own institution’s formula.
  • Using × 10 where your university subtracts an offset. A plain × 10 on a VTU CGPA of 8.2 gives 82% instead of 74.5% — a difference big enough to claim an eligibility you do not have. If your calculated figure looks a point or two high, an offset is usually the reason.
  • Rounding the CGPA before converting. Rounding 7.86 to 7.9 before multiplying by 9.5 turns 74.67% into 75.05% and crosses a 75% cut-off that the real figure does not. Convert the CGPA exactly as printed, and round only the percentage, the way your institution says to.
  • Quoting your own conversion as if it were official. An application that asks for a percentage usually wants the figure your institution issues, or your CGPA with its conversion rule attached. A number you worked out yourself is useful for checking eligibility, and is not evidence of it.
  • Mixing up SGPA and CGPA. An SGPA covers one semester; a CGPA covers all of them. Converting your best semester’s SGPA and quoting it as your overall percentage is an easy mistake to make on a form and an awkward one to explain at verification.

How long this should take

Seconds once you know your institution’s formula. Finding that formula is the real work, and it is usually printed on the back of the marksheet or in the examination regulations.

Frequently asked questions

What is 7.5 CGPA in percentage?

Under a plain ten-point rule, 75%. Under CBSE’s rule, 7.5 × 9.5 = 71.25%. Under GTU it is 70% and under VTU 67.5%. Which one is right depends entirely on who issued the CGPA.

Is CGPA × 10 the correct formula?

For some universities, yes; for many, no. It is the most common default, but CBSE uses 9.5 and several universities subtract an offset first. Check your own marksheet or regulations.

What if my university uses a conversion table, not a formula?

Then use the table. No formula can reproduce a table, and a calculator that pretends to will be wrong somewhere.

Can a converted percentage be more than 100?

With some formulas, at the very top of the scale, yes — it is a property of the formula rather than a mistake. The converter shows the highest figure your chosen formula can produce so you can see it.

Is the converted figure accepted on applications?

Only if it follows your institution’s published rule, and often only alongside the CGPA itself. Quote what is on your transcript, and attach the conversion rule if a form insists on a percentage.
